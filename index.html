The Problem [cite: 35]

Sports analytics present a two-fold problem because they are not designed for consumers[cite: 36, 37].

Problem 1: Not Aimed at Consumers [cite: 38, 39]

  • Leagues & Teams focus on player performance, injury prediction, and strategic optimization[cite: 41].
  • Gambling Companies focus on odds setting, risk management, and customer profiling[cite: 42].

Problem 2: Aimed at High-Skilled Users [cite: 43, 44]

  • Academic resources use specific metrics for measuring results (WAR, RAPM, AP)[cite: 46].
  • These are academic exercises, not actionable insights[cite: 47].
  • Existing DIY setups leave model building to users, expecting statistical or mathematical knowledge[cite: 50, 51].
  • Analysts often choose to stay at an arms length to gambling, requiring the user to do their own analysis to make betting decisions[cite: 55, 56].
  • Existing B2C products are niche, typically focusing on specific sports or specific data sets instead of broadly scoped products[cite: 52, 53, 54].

The Solution [cite: 57]

Analytics for Consumers: Professional-grade models, consumer-friendly delivery [cite: 58, 59]
  • In-House Models: Proprietary AI/ML models developed internally[cite: 62, 63, 64].
  • Specific Outputs: Focuses on Spread and Total Points, not abstract metrics[cite: 65, 66].
  • Zero Expertise Required: No statistical or mathematical knowledge is needed[cite: 67, 68].
  • Consumer UI/UX: Designed for ease of information access[cite: 69, 70].
  • Simple Subscription: Features no complex pricing or hidden fees[cite: 71, 72].
  • 1:1 Model to Expectation: Direct, understandable outputs[cite: 73].
